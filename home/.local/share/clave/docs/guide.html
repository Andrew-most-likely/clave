Start here

Clave is a desktop for Hyprland on Arch Linux. Hyprland still tiles your windows; Clave adds a menu bar, a Dock, Control Center, Notification Center, an Overview of windows and Spaces, System Settings, a login screen, a boot splash and title bar buttons. Most of it is changed in System Settings (Super+,).

  • Super is the key with the Windows or command symbol. Menus show it as ⌘ (or ❖, System Settings > Keyboard).
  • Press Super+/ at any time for the full list of shortcuts.
  • Press Super+Space to find an app, a file or a setting, or to calculate.

Desktop

Menu bar

One bar on every screen. On the left: the logo menu (About This Computer, System Settings, Software, Force Quit, Sleep, Restart, Shut Down, Lock Screen, Log Out), then the name and menus of the app in front. On the right: app status icons, battery, Wi-Fi, sound, Control Center and the clock. Click the clock for Notification Center. Battery percentage, date, day and 24-hour clock are in System Settings > Control Center.

Dock

Running apps have a dot under them. Right-click an icon to pin it to the Dock or unpin it, or to open a new window. Minimized windows go into the Dock (Super+M). Size, magnification, position and hiding are in System Settings > Desktop & Dock.

Overview and Spaces

Super+Tab, or a three-finger swipe up, shows every window and Space. Super+1…0 goes to a Space; Super+Shift+1…0 moves the window there. Swipe left and right with three fingers to change Space. Hot corners (System Settings > Desktop & Dock) can open Overview, Notification Center, Control Center or Apps, lock the screen or put the display to sleep.

Control Center

The switch icon in the menu bar, or Super+Ctrl+N. Click a circle to turn Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Ethernet, VPN, Focus (Do Not Disturb), Night Light or Mirroring on or off; click the text next to it to open its settings. Below: brightness, volume, Light or Dark mode, keyboard brightness and what is playing.

Notification Center

Click the clock or press Super+N. Banners stay for the time set in System Settings > Notifications, where you also pick the notification sound, turn on Do Not Disturb and mute single apps.

Search and Apps

Super+Space (or a tap of Super) opens Search: type an app name, or a sum such as 12*7; Ctrl+Tab switches to the calculator, Enter copies the result. Super+A or a four-finger pinch opens the Apps grid.

Windows

Windows tile by default. Super+T floats or tiles the window in front, Super+F makes it full screen, Super+Arrows moves focus, Super+Alt+Arrows swaps windows and Super+Shift+Arrows resizes.

Title bar buttons

Apps without their own title bar get a bar with red (close), yellow (minimize into the Dock) and green (full screen) buttons. Double-click the bar to zoom. The bar follows Light and Dark mode. Turn the buttons off in System Settings > Desktop & Dock > Windows.

An app shows two sets of buttons? It draws its own title bar. While it is open, pick it under "App shows two sets of buttons?" in System Settings > Desktop & Dock > Windows; Clave then leaves its bar off. Remove it there to undo. Clave's own windows (System Settings, Calendar, Activity Monitor) are never on this list.

Text size changes Clave's windows (System Settings, Calendar, Activity Monitor) and GTK apps at once. The menu bar, Dock and Control Center keep their size. Qt apps follow when reopened. The terminal has its own font size in ~/.config/kitty/custom.conf.

Apps

Clave installs one app per job from the Arch repositories: Files, Text Editor, Calculator, Image Viewer, Document Viewer, Archive Manager, Clock, Videos, Camera, Console, System Information, Disk Utility, Keychain and Backups. Clave's own apps are Calendar (plain .ics files, no accounts), Activity Monitor and Force Quit. More apps install through Software (Flathub).

Screenshots and recording

Print opens the toolbar: a window, an area or the whole screen, as a picture or a recording. Shift+Print copies the text inside an area (no network). Options are in the toolbar.

Terminal

The terminal is kitty (Super+Return). Clave's shell setup is ~/.local/share/clave/clave.bash, loaded by your ~/.bashrc. Put your own settings in ~/.bashrc; Clave never replaces it. Commands it adds:

Security

Unless you installed with --no-harden, Clave hardens the system: the linux-hardened kernel, a firewall that drops incoming connections (nftables), OpenSnitch for outgoing connections, AppArmor, auditd, USBGuard, three wrong passwords lock the account for 15 minutes, stricter sudo, a noexec /tmp, an AIDE file baseline and daily arch-audit checks for known vulnerabilities.

USB accessories

USBGuard blocks USB devices that were not connected at installation. A notification says when one is blocked. System Settings > Privacy & Security > USB accessories lists every device with Allow or Block; the choice is kept for the next time it is plugged in, and it asks for your password. Keyboards, mice and hubs cannot be blocked there, so you cannot lock yourself out.

OpenSnitch

The first time an app connects to the internet, OpenSnitch asks whether to allow it. Without an answer it denies after 30 seconds. Change the rules in OpenSnitch (System Settings > Privacy & Security).

Network Identity

Off by default. Makes this computer look like another common device on shared networks, or answer nothing new (Stealth). See How Network Identity works.

Disk encryption

System Settings > Privacy & Security says whether your files are encrypted. How to encrypt, and how to get back in when locked out: __REPO__/docs/ENCRYPTION.md and __REPO__/docs/RECOVERY.md. Keep a recovery USB.

Updating

System Settings > General > Software Update, or clave-update in a terminal. It updates system packages and Flatpaks, then installs the newest signed Clave release. Hyprland plugins (title bars, minimize) are rebuilt after each Hyprland upgrade. clave-doctor says whether the installed packages work with this Clave release.

Troubleshooting

  • Something looks wrong after an update: run clave-doctor.
  • No title bar buttons: the plugin may not match your Hyprland. Clave offers a rebuild at login, or run ~/.local/share/clave/rebuild-plugins.sh. Log: ~/.cache/clave/rebuild-plugins.log.
  • A USB device does nothing: it is blocked; allow it in Privacy & Security.
  • An app cannot reach the internet: OpenSnitch denied it; change the rule in OpenSnitch.
  • Locked out: wait 15 minutes after three wrong passwords, or see __REPO__/docs/RECOVERY.md.
  • Installer log: ~/.cache/clave/install.log. Attach it and the plugin log to bug reports.

Uninstalling

__REPO__/uninstall.sh            # files in your home folder
__REPO__/uninstall.sh --system   # also the login screen, boot splash and hardening

Your earlier files come back from the backups. Installed packages stay; remove them with sudo pacman -Rs if you want.
